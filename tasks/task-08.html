Model reference

The comparison in three equations.

Mismatch means different outcomes at the two detectors. Each output remains on the same 0–1 probability scale.

Supplied classical model

Independent outcomes

PC = 1 − cos²θ cos²φ − sin²θ sin²φ

The detector probabilities are combined independently and retain their separate orientations to the fixed reference axis.

Quantum model

Entangled correlation

PQ = sin²(φ − θ)

The Bell-pair prediction depends only on the relative detector angle δ = φ − θ.

Signed contrast

Where models separate

ΔP = −½ sin(2θ) sin(2φ)

The contrast lies between −0.5 and +0.5; percentage points avoid division by zero at a classical probability of zero.

Required example

θ = −30° · φ = +30° · δ = +60°

PC = 3/8 = 37.5% PQ = 3/4 = 75.0%
Model boundary

Ideal polarization analyzers and a perfect entangled pair are assumed. Loss, detector inefficiency, dark counts, decoherence, basis sifting and finite-key security are outside this calculator.
