Read the first curve carefully. The angular shape is universal, but its scale is set by λC/λ. The recoil-angle curve also shifts when the photon energy changes. The energy ledger above stays balanced at every selected point.

Method

Conservation writes the curve.

Start with the photon momentum h/λ, keep the electron initially at rest, and do not replace the electron’s relativistic speed with a non-relativistic estimate.

01 / wavelength

Compton’s relation

λ′ − λ = h/mec (1 − cos θ)

The constant h/(mec) is the electron Compton wavelength, λC = 2.426 pm. A larger angle means a larger loss of photon momentum and a longer outgoing wavelength.

02 / energy

Give the difference to the electron

Ke = hc (1/λ − 1/λ′)

The photon’s energy loss becomes the electron’s kinetic energy. The plotted speed follows γ = 1 + Ke/(mec²) and v/c = √(1 − γ−2).

03 / momentum

Resolve the recoil

tan φ = sin θ/(1 + λC/λ)(1 − cos θ)

φ is measured below the incoming x direction, as in the task diagram. At θ → 0 the recoil energy vanishes, while the direction approaches a right angle as a limiting direction.
