Method

From stationary states to one photon

These are ideal calculated vacuum wavelengths, not measured line centres. Integer quantum numbers select the states and a downward change releases the positive energy difference as one photon.
Ideal hydrogen level

En = − hcR∞ n²

ER = hcR∞ = 13.605693 eV. Every finite bound-state energy is negative and approaches 0 from below.
Emission energy

Eγ = Enᵢ − En_f > 0

Photon relation

Eγ = hf = hc λ

Rydberg form

1 λ = R∞ ( 1 n_f² − 1 nᵢ² )

Series map

nf = 1 Lyman · 2 Balmer · 3 Paschen · 4 Brackett · 5 Pfund

Worked model anchor

H-α is 3→2: Eγ = 1.889 eV and λ = 656.112 nm in this infinite-nucleus model.

Before ni > nf

The atom begins in the less tightly bound, higher level.

Difference ΔEatom = −Eγ

The atom loses energy; the emitted photon carries a positive amount.

After λ = hc / Eγ

A larger level gap means a higher-energy, shorter-wavelength photon.

Model boundary

This page uses ideal, non-relativistic hydrogen with a stationary, infinitely massive proton and vacuum wavelengths. Reduced mass, fine and hyperfine structure, Lamb shifts, fields, line strengths, selection rules, and broadening are deliberately outside the accepted baseline.
