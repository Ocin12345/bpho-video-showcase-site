Method

From voltage to ring radius

The baseline follows the non-relativistic derivation for electron-wave rings from graphite layer spacings, then projects each allowed scattering angle onto the phosphor screen.
Electron wavelength

λ = h √(2meeV)

λ ∝ V−1/2. The accepted model is non-relativistic and uses exact SI h and e with the frozen 2022 CODATA electron mass.
Bragg condition

2d sin θ = nλ

d is graphite plane spacing; n is the positive integer diffraction order; θ is the Bragg glancing angle. Here q ≡ nλ/(2d) = sin θ = sin(φ/2).
Screen radius

x = r sin φ

With φ = 2 sin−1(nλ / 2d), the spherical-screen radius is measured from the image centre to the ring.
Screen radius x = r sin φ

Here φ = 2θ is the total electron scattering angle and r = 65 mm is the spherical diffraction-tube radius. This gives the distance from the image centre to the ring on the phosphor screen.

Equivalent full diameter y = 2r sin φ = 2x

A caliper measurement across the complete ring gives the diameter y. It is exactly twice the screen radius x for the same scattering angle.

Model notes and assumptions

Angle convention: θ is the Bragg glancing angle and φ = 2θ is the total electron scattering angle. The screen geometry is x = r sin φ; the corresponding full ring diameter is y = 2x. The calculation uses the exact inverse sine with no small-angle approximation.

Model boundary

The calculation predicts ideal geometric positions and order domains. Ring intensity, graphite structure factors, broadening, beam spread, magnetic deflection, multiple scattering, phosphor response, camera distortion, and relativistic momentum are excluded from the official baseline. Assumptions are single elastic scattering, fixed graphite layer spacings, a 65 mm spherical glass diffraction tube, and a non-relativistic electron accelerated from rest. Measurement noise is not simulated. Relativity is tested separately below.
