The displayed standing wave is a stationary energy eigenstate. Its complex time phase changes, but |ψ|² remains fixed.

01 / Numerical evidence

The uncertainty trade-off is visible.

Changing the width rescales Δx and Δp in opposite directions, while changing n raises the energy and the uncertainty product.

02 / The model

Boundary conditions choose the states.

The infinite walls force the wavefunction to vanish at x = 0 and x = a, so only an integer number of half-wavelengths fits inside.

01 / wavefunction

Normalised standing wave

ψₙ(x) = √(2/a) sin(nπx/a)

The probability density is |ψₙ|². The state has n − 1 interior nodes and n − 1 excited-state energy gaps above the ground state.

02 / energy

Quantised kinetic energy

Eₙ = n²π²ℏ² / (2ma²)

Higher n means shorter de Broglie wavelength and larger momentum. A narrower well raises every energy as 1/a².

03 / uncertainty

Position and momentum

Δx Δp = (ℏ/2)√(n²π²/3 − 2)

For a stationary state ⟨x⟩ = a/2 and ⟨p⟩ = 0, but Δp is non-zero because the standing wave contains equal opposite momentum components.
